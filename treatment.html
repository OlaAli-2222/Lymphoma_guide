PATIENT & CAREGIVER GUIDE

Understanding treatment options

Lymphoma treatment is not one-size-fits-all. Learn the broad categories and prepare questions for your specialist team.

Do not use this page to choose or change treatment.

The right plan depends on the exact subtype, pathology, stage, disease behavior, previous treatment, overall health, and personal circumstances. Do not delay or stop prescribed treatment based on online information.

Common treatment categories

Chemotherapy

Medicines that damage or stop the growth of cancer cells. Regimens and side effects differ by diagnosis.

Immunotherapy

Treatments that use or change immune-system activity to help fight cancer, including some antibody-based treatments.

Targeted therapy

Medicines aimed at particular proteins or pathways involved in cancer-cell growth.

Radiation therapy

Focused treatment that may be used in selected situations, depending on the lymphoma and treatment plan.

Stem cell transplant or cellular therapy

Specialized approaches considered for certain diagnoses or situations; eligibility and risks need expert discussion.

Active monitoring

For selected slow-growing lymphomas, a specialist may recommend careful monitoring before treatment begins. This is not appropriate for every type.

Questions to discuss with your team

  • What is the goal of treatment in my situation?
  • Why is this approach recommended for my exact subtype?
  • What benefits, side effects, and uncertainties should I understand?
  • How might treatment affect work, study, fertility, or daily activities?
  • Which symptoms during treatment need an urgent call?
  • Are there reasonable alternatives or relevant clinical trials?
  • Who should I contact outside clinic hours?

Side effects and supportive care

Ask your team for written instructions about infection precautions, medicines, symptom management, and whom to contact. Do not rely on general online lists to decide whether a symptom is safe to ignore.

Bring a second person if you want

A trusted family member or friend can help take notes, remember questions, and discuss the information with you afterwards.
