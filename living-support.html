PATIENT & CAREGIVER GUIDE

Living with lymphoma & finding support

Practical ideas for patients, families, and caregivers before, during, and after treatment.

You do not have to manage everything at once

People respond to a diagnosis in different ways. Some want detailed information immediately; others prefer one question at a time. You can tell your care team how much detail feels useful and ask them to repeat or write things down.

Practical ways to prepare

Keep a care folder

Store reports, medication lists, appointment dates, questions, and contact details in one place.

Bring support

Ask someone you trust to join appointments if you want help listening, note-taking, or remembering next steps.

Ask about daily life

Discuss sleep, nutrition, movement, work or study, emotional wellbeing, and infection precautions with your clinical team.

Clarify who to call

Get a written plan for symptoms, medication questions, and after-hours concerns.

Supporting someone with lymphoma

  • Ask what kind of help would be useful rather than assuming.
  • Offer specific practical support, such as transport, meals, or taking notes.
  • Respect privacy and the patient’s choices about sharing information.
  • Encourage questions for the clinical team without making treatment decisions for the patient.
  • Caregivers also deserve rest, support, and someone to talk to.

Finding reliable support

Look for organizations that explain their funding, protect privacy, distinguish evidence from personal stories, and do not promise guaranteed cures. Peer groups can provide connection, but another person's experience may not apply to your diagnosis.

Be cautious with miracle-cure claims

Do not replace or delay recommended medical care because of testimonials, supplements, or online advice. Discuss complementary approaches and possible interactions with your care team.

For local services, ask your hospital social worker or care team what is available in your area. Service availability and eligibility can change.
