Types of lymphoma

Lymphoma is a broad term. The exact diagnosis matters because different subtypes can behave differently and may need different approaches.

One important point

“Lymphoma” alone is not a complete diagnosis. Ask your team for the exact subtype and a copy of the pathology report. Treatment choices should be made with a specialist team.

The two broad groups

Group 1

Hodgkin lymphoma (HL)

A group of lymphomas defined by specific features on pathology testing. There are different subtypes, and care depends on the precise diagnosis and stage.

Read the NCI patient guide →
Group 2

Non-Hodgkin lymphoma (NHL)

A large collection of lymphomas, including B-cell, T-cell, and NK-cell types. Some grow slowly; others can grow quickly.

Read the NCI patient guide →

Examples of non-Hodgkin lymphoma subtypes

Diffuse large B-cell lymphoma (DLBCL)

A common aggressive B-cell lymphoma. The treatment plan depends on pathology findings, stage, and individual factors.

Follicular lymphoma

Often slow-growing, though behavior and treatment needs vary. Some people may be monitored before treatment is needed.

Mantle cell lymphoma

A B-cell lymphoma with specific diagnostic features. Treatment is individualized and may involve several approaches.

Marginal zone lymphoma

A group of related lymphomas that can arise in different tissues, including lymph nodes, spleen, or mucosal sites.

Burkitt lymphoma

A fast-growing lymphoma that needs prompt specialist assessment and treatment planning.

Peripheral T-cell lymphomas

A varied group of uncommon lymphomas that arise from T cells and require subtype-specific assessment.

Do not self-classify from symptoms

Swollen lymph nodes, fever, night sweats, and fatigue can have many causes. Only an appropriate medical evaluation and diagnostic testing can determine whether lymphoma is present.

What to look for in your report

  • The full diagnostic name and subtype.
  • Pathology and immunohistochemistry findings, where applicable.
  • Any recommended additional tests or specialist review.
  • Who to contact if the report wording is unclear.
