PATIENT & CAREGIVER GUIDE

Diagnosis & tests

Learn what common tests are for and how diagnosis and staging are usually discussed with a clinical team.

Tests answer different questions

A blood test alone usually cannot confirm or rule out lymphoma. The tests needed depend on symptoms, examination, and the suspected diagnosis.

1. Clinical assessment

Your team may ask about symptoms, medical history, medicines, and how long a swelling or other symptom has been present. They may examine lymph nodes and other areas.

2. Biopsy and pathology

A biopsy collects tissue so a pathologist can examine it. Depending on the situation, this may involve removing all or part of a lymph node or taking a tissue sample from another area. The best approach is decided by the clinical team.

Additional laboratory methods, such as immunohistochemistry, flow cytometry, or genetic tests, may help identify the exact subtype. Not every patient needs every test.

3. Blood tests and imaging

Blood tests can provide information about general health and help guide care. Imaging such as CT or PET/CT may be used to assess where lymphoma is located and help plan treatment. Which tests are appropriate varies by case.

4. Staging

Staging describes the extent of disease based on the tests and the type of lymphoma. Stage is only one part of the picture; subtype, biology, symptoms, overall health, and other factors can also matter.

5. Questions to ask

  • Has the diagnosis been confirmed, or are more results pending?
  • What is the exact subtype written in the pathology report?
  • Do I need a specialist pathology review?
  • Which tests are recommended, and what will each result change?
  • When and how will someone explain the results to me?
Seek urgent medical advice when needed

Contact your care team or local urgent service for severe, sudden, or rapidly worsening symptoms. This page is not an emergency assessment tool.
